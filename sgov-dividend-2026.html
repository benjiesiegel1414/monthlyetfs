3 Patterns in SGOV's Payout Calendar

  • The March payment tends to dip. SGOV's March distribution mostly reflects interest earned in February, the shortest month. March 2026 paid $0.2724, the lowest of the year so far, and March 2023 paid $0.278, the lowest that year.
  • December pays twice. There is a regular early-December payment plus a second one in the middle or late part of the month (December 19 in 2025, December 18 in 2024, December 14 in 2023). That second payment helps the fund distribute all of its income for the calendar year.
  • There is no January ex-date. Because of the extra December payment, the next one after that lands in early February. You still get 12 payments a year, just not one in every calendar month.

Why SGOV's Dividend Moves With Interest Rates

$SGOV holds U.S. Treasury bills that mature in three months or less. Because those bills roll over constantly, the fund's income resets to current short-term rates within weeks. When T-bill yields rise, $SGOV's monthly payout rises soon after. When they fall, it falls just as fast.

That makes $SGOV one of the purest ways to collect the current short-term interest rate in a brokerage account. It does not try to beat T-bills with options, leverage or credit risk. That is also why its yield sits far below the covered call funds you will see on our highest yield monthly ETFs list. Those funds take on market risk to pay more. $SGOV pays the risk-free rate, minus a 0.09% expense ratio.

Does SGOV's Price Go Down?

Not in the way price decay hurts many high-yield income funds. $SGOV's share price follows a tight saw-tooth pattern: it creeps up a few cents each day as interest accrues, then drops by roughly the dividend amount on the ex-date. Over the past 52 weeks it traded between $100.27 and $100.73. Its one-year total return of 3.79% includes those distributions.

⚠️ Don't try to "capture" the SGOV dividend. Buying the day before the ex-date and selling right after usually nets you nothing, because the price falls by about the amount of the dividend on the ex-date. And for most holders, the dividend is taxed as ordinary income.

How SGOV Dividends Are Taxed

$SGOV's distributions come from Treasury bill interest, so they are generally taxed as ordinary income at the federal level. They are not qualified dividends. The good news for many investors is that interest from U.S. Treasury obligations is often exempt from state and local income tax. How much of that exemption flows through an ETF depends on your state's rules and on the share of income iShares reports as coming from Treasuries each year.

If you live in a high-tax state, that state exemption can make $SGOV's after-tax yield more competitive with a bank account or money market fund than the headline numbers suggest. Always check iShares' annual tax supplement and talk to a tax professional before relying on it.

SGOV Dividend FAQ

Does SGOV pay monthly dividends?

Yes. $SGOV pays every month, usually going ex-dividend on the first business day and paying three to five business days later. December has two payments and January has none, so you still get 12 payments a year.

How much does SGOV pay per month?

$SGOV paid $0.30055 per share for October 2026. Over the last 12 months it averaged about $0.31 per share per month. At a $100.44 share price, that is roughly $30 a month for every $10,000 invested, before taxes.

When is SGOV's next ex-dividend date?

The next ex-dividend date is expected to be Monday, November 2, 2026, with payment around November 5. You need to own shares before the ex-date to receive that payment.

How often does SGOV pay dividends?

Twelve times a year. $SGOV skips a January ex-date because it makes two payments in December.

What is SGOV's dividend yield?

About 3.65% based on the $3.67 paid over the last 12 months and the October 2, 2026 price of $100.44. The yield moves with short-term Treasury rates.

Is SGOV's dividend taxed as ordinary income?

Generally yes at the federal level, since the income comes from T-bill interest rather than qualified dividends. Treasury interest is often exempt from state and local income tax, but the rules vary by state. Check iShares' tax information and a tax professional.

Why is SGOV paying less than last year?

Because short-term rates were lower. $SGOV paid $3.67 per share over the last 12 months versus $4.30 in the prior 12 months, a drop of about 14.7%. Monthly payouts have been steady near $0.30 since spring 2026.

Does SGOV's share price go down?

Only by small amounts on each ex-date. Its price rises slightly through the month as interest accrues, then drops by about the dividend amount on the ex-date. The 52-week range was $100.27 to $100.73.
