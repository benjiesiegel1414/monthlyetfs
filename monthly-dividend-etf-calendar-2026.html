Quick Answer

Monthly dividend ETFs don't all pay on the same day. Most fall into four windows. $SGOV, $BIL, $JEPI, $JEPQ, $GPIX and $GPIQ go ex-dividend on the first business day of the month and pay 2 to 5 business days later. $CSHI goes ex in the second week. $QYLD, $XYLD, $SPHD and the NEOS funds ($SPYI, $QQQI, $BTCI) go ex in the third week. And $DIVO, $QDVO, $KNG, $FTQI and $SVOL pay at the end of the month.

One of the most common questions I get is some version of "when does this thing actually pay?" When does $SGOV pay. When does $BTCI pay. When does $SPHD pay. I see those searches land on this site every single week.

So instead of answering it one fund at a time, here's the whole calendar in one place. I pulled the last four payments for 23 of the most popular monthly ETFs and sorted them by when they really go ex-dividend and when the cash shows up.

Here's the part most people miss: the schedules are not random. Each fund family runs its own rhythm, and once you know the rhythm you can predict next month's dates pretty well.

Ex-Date vs Pay Date: The Only Two Dates That Matter

Before the calendar, let's get the two dates straight, because people mix them up constantly.

  • Ex-dividend date: you need to own the shares before this day to get the payment. Buy on the ex-date or after, and that month's dividend goes to the seller.
  • Pay date: the day the cash actually lands in your brokerage account. Usually 1 to 9 days after the ex-date for the funds below.

There's also a record date, but with today's one-day settlement it's almost always the same day as the ex-date for these funds. You can ignore it.

And one more thing: on the ex-date, the share price usually drops by about the amount of the dividend. You're not getting free money by buying the day before. That's why I never try to "game" ex-dates. I care about the calendar for planning cash flow, not for trading.

Early Month Payers: $SGOV, $BIL, $JEPI, $GPIX

This is the biggest group, and it's where most of the money in monthly ETFs actually sits.

Treasury funds: $SGOV and $BIL

$SGOV and $BIL both go ex-dividend on the first business day of the month. In 2026 that has meant July 1, August 3, September 1 and October 1. The cash then arrives a few business days later: July 7, August 6, September 4 and October 6.

One thing that confuses people: the payment you get in early October is really September's interest. These funds collect T-bill interest all month, then pay it out right after the month ends. And if you're wondering about state taxes on that income, I covered that in is $SGOV state tax exempt.

JPMorgan: $JEPI, $JEPQ and $JPIE

Same ex-date as $SGOV, but JPMorgan pays a little faster. October's ex-date was October 1 and the cash landed October 5. In September it was the 1st and the 3rd. If you want your income in the first week of the month, these are about as early as it gets.

Goldman Sachs: $GPIX and $GPIQ

Also ex on the first business day, but the Goldman funds pay a few days later than JPMorgan, around the 7th or 8th. In October: ex October 1, paid October 7. I went deeper on both in my $GPIX dividend guide and $GPIQ dividend guide.

Mid-Month and Third Week: $CSHI, $QYLD, NEOS

$CSHI: the mid-month T-bill fund

$CSHI is the odd one out in the NEOS lineup. It holds T-bills plus a small options strategy, and it goes ex on a Wednesday about a week before the rest of NEOS. Its last four ex-dates were July 15, August 12, September 9 and October 14, with payment two days later each time. That makes it a nice way to fill the gap between the early month Treasury funds and the third week crowd. Live numbers are on the $CSHI page.

Global X and Invesco: Monday ex-dates

$QYLD, $XYLD and $RYLD all go ex-dividend on a Monday in the third or fourth week. In 2026: July 20, August 24, September 21 and October 19. They pay on the Thursday of that same week. $SPHD from Invesco runs on the same Monday, but pays on Friday instead.

NEOS: Wednesday ex-dates

$SPYI, $QQQI, $BTCI, $IWMI and $MLPI all go ex on a Wednesday in the third or fourth week and pay that Friday. Recent ex-dates: July 22, August 19, September 16 and October 21. I checked the July dates against NEOS's own distribution announcement and they line up.

This is why so many people searching "when does $BTCI pay dividends" get confused. It's not the first of the month like $SGOV. It's later, and it moves around a bit from month to month because it's tied to a weekday, not a calendar date.

End of Month Payers: $DIVO, $QDVO, $KNG, $SVOL

The last group pays right at the end of the month, which is handy if your bills are due on the 1st.

  • $DIVO and $QDVO (Amplify): ex on the second to last business day, paid the very last business day. June 29 and 30, July 30 and 31, August 28 and 31, September 29 and 30. Expect October's right at the end of this week.
  • $KNG (FT Vest) and $FTQI (First Trust): ex around the 21st to 24th, but they don't pay until the last business day. That's the longest gap on this list, 6 to 9 calendar days between ex-date and cash.
  • $SVOL (Simplify): ex in the last week, paid the last business day. Also the most boring payout here, in a good way: $0.280 a share four months straight.

3 Calendar Mistakes I See All the Time

1. Buying the day before ex-date to "grab" the dividend. The price drops by roughly the payout on the ex-date. On a fund like $BTCI paying about $0.70 a share, that drop is very real. You're just turning your own money into a taxable distribution.

2. Confusing the ex-date with the pay date. If you sell on October 2, you still get $SGOV's October payment, because you owned it on the October 1 ex-date. Lots of people panic-sell or wait to sell for no reason.

3. Assuming the amount stays the same. The calendar is predictable. The amount is not. $JEPQ paid $0.70497 in August and $0.56687 in October. $XYLD dropped from $0.4088 in July to around $0.31. Option income and interest rates move, so payouts move. Budget off an average, not your best month.

My Take

Knowing when your funds pay is genuinely useful. It helps you plan bills, time reinvestment, and avoid dumb mistakes around ex-dates. But it's the least important thing about an income ETF.

I'd rather own a fund that pays on an "inconvenient" date and holds its value than chase a fund because it fills a gap in my calendar and slowly bleeds NAV. Build the portfolio on quality first. Then let the calendar be a nice bonus.

Monthly Dividend ETF Calendar FAQ

What day of the month does $SGOV pay dividends?

$SGOV goes ex-dividend on the first business day of each month and pays a few business days later. In 2026 its payments were July 7, August 6, September 4 and October 6, after ex-dates of July 1, August 3, September 1 and October 1.

When does $BTCI pay dividends?

$BTCI, like the other NEOS income funds such as $SPYI and $QQQI, goes ex-dividend on a Wednesday in the third or fourth week of the month and pays that Friday. Its October 2026 payment of $0.6954 per share went ex October 21 and paid October 23.

When does $SPHD pay dividends?

$SPHD goes ex-dividend on a Monday in the third or fourth week of the month and pays that Friday. Its October 2026 payment of $0.2014 per share went ex October 19 and paid October 23.

Do I get the dividend if I sell on the ex-dividend date?

Yes. If you owned the shares before the ex-dividend date, you get that month's payment even if you sell on the ex-date or any time after. If you buy on the ex-date, the payment goes to the seller.

Can I get paid every week with monthly dividend ETFs?

Roughly, yes. Combining funds from different pay windows, such as $JEPI or $SGOV early in the month, $CSHI mid-month, $SPYI or $QYLD in the third week and $KNG or $DIVO at month end, spreads payments across most weeks. Funds that pay weekly on their own are tracked at WeeklyETFs.com.
