My $SGOV dividend guide is still the most read page on this site, and I've since done the same deep dive on $BIL, $USFR and $SHV. $TBIL is the last of the big Treasury cash funds, and it's the simplest one of all: it basically owns one Treasury bill at a time.

If you hold $TBIL and noticed your dividend now shows up at the end of the month instead of the start, or you're wondering whether it pays more than $SGOV, this is for you.

What Is $TBIL?

$TBIL is the F/m US Treasury 3 Month Bill ETF, run by F/m Investments. Its official name today is the F/m US Treasury 3 Month Bill Fund, ETF Class Shares, because it's now the ETF share class of a fund in The RBB Fund, Inc. trust. It launched on August 9, 2022, trades on Nasdaq, and had about $7.51 billion in net assets as of November 13, 2026.

It tracks the Bloomberg US Treasury Bellwether 3M Index, which follows the most recently auctioned, or "on-the-run," 3-month Treasury bill. Here's the part most people miss: instead of holding a ladder of bills like $SGOV or $BIL, $TBIL holds the current 3-month bill and rolls into the new one as the Treasury auctions it.

  • Very few holdings. On its September 30, 2026 fact sheet, 99.99% of the fund sat in a single Treasury bill maturing December 24, 2026. F/m's site listed 4 holdings on November 13, as it rolls between bills.
  • Effective duration of 0.23 years on September 30, so the price barely reacts to rate moves.
  • Expense ratio of 0.15%, or $15 a year per $10,000. No 12b-1 fee and no other expenses, per the prospectus.
  • Tight trading. F/m reported a median 30-day bid/ask spread of 0.02% and a 0.01% premium on November 13.

The price tells the story. $TBIL's NAV was $49.93 on November 13, 2026, versus $49.85 on September 30. Like every T-bill fund, the interest builds up in the share price during the month and then gets paid out to you, so the price just drifts up a few cents and drops back on the ex-date.

How Much Does $TBIL Pay Per Month?

Over the last 12 payments, from December 2025 through October 2026, $TBIL paid between $0.14696 and $0.16705 per share. The October payment of $0.16705 was the highest of the bunch, and spring 2026 was the low point.

Real dollars: $10,000 buys about 200 shares at $49.93. At the October rate, that's about $33.46 for the month. Over the last 12 payments, the same $10,000 would have collected about $366.

You'll see two yield numbers, and they measure different things:

  • Trailing 12-month yield: about 3.66%. The last 12 payments ($1.83) divided by the $49.93 price. What actually got paid.
  • 30-day SEC yield: 3.92% as of October 31, 2026. A forward-looking snapshot of what the current bill is earning after fees.

F/m's own dividend report also shows a 4.01% distribution rate, which is just October's payout times 12 divided by the price. All three are in the same neighborhood, which is what you'd expect from a fund that owns one 3-month bill.

$TBIL Ex-Dividend Dates: The Switch to Month-End

This is the part that confuses people. Through most of 2025, $TBIL went ex-dividend on the first business day of the month, just like $SGOV and $BIL. Starting at the end of 2025, it moved to the last week of the month.

That's why December 2025 had two payments: one with an ex-date of December 2 and another with an ex-date of December 30. Since then, every payment has landed in the final days of the month, with the pay date one business day after the ex-date.

Are $TBIL Dividends Taxable?

Yes, at the federal level. $TBIL's dividends come from Treasury interest, so they're taxed as ordinary income, not at the lower qualified dividend rate. The prospectus shows how big that bite can be: the 2025 return was 4.21% before taxes but 2.50% after taxes on distributions, using the highest federal rate.

At the state level, interest from U.S. Treasuries is generally exempt from state and local income tax, and $TBIL holds essentially nothing but Treasury bills. F/m publishes supplemental tax information each year that shows how much of the income qualifies. I walked through how this works in is $SGOV state tax exempt, and the same logic applies to $TBIL. This is not tax advice, so talk to a tax pro if it matters for you, especially in a high-tax state like California or New York.

The Real Risks

  • Falling rates. The big one. $TBIL's payout tracks the 3-month T-bill rate. You can see it in the history: payments dropped from about $0.188 in July 2025 to about $0.147 in spring 2026.
  • A higher fee than $SGOV. 0.15% versus 0.09%. Small, but you pay it every single year.
  • Inflation and taxes. A yield under 4%, taxed as ordinary income, doesn't leave much after inflation. This is a parking spot, not a growth plan.
  • Not a bank account. It's not FDIC insured, and the price moves a few cents around each payout. It can also trade at a small premium or discount to NAV.

What's not really a risk: price decay. A fund that owns a single 3-month bill has almost no room for its price to fall apart. NAV went from $49.85 at the end of September to $49.93 on November 13, which is just interest building up before the next payout.

My Take

I've been a dividend investor for over 10 years, and I reached financial freedom at 31 by building income. $TBIL goes in the same bucket as $SGOV, $BIL, $USFR and $SHV: it's the cash drawer, not the engine.

If I were picking one Treasury cash fund for new money, I'd still lean $SGOV for the lower fee. But $TBIL has a real use: its month-end payday. $TBIL Dividend FAQ

Does $TBIL pay dividends monthly?

Yes. $TBIL pays a dividend every month. Since the end of 2025 it goes ex-dividend in the last week of the month and pays the next business day. The October 2026 dividend went ex on October 27 and was paid October 28.

How much does $TBIL pay per month?

The October 2026 payment was $0.16705 per share. Over the last 12 payments $TBIL paid about $1.83 per share, roughly 3.66% on its $49.93 price. That works out to about $29 to $33 a month for every $10,000 invested.

When is $TBIL's next dividend?

F/m Investments' 2026 schedule shows the next $TBIL ex-dividend date on November 24, 2026, paid November 25, and the December dividend going ex on December 29 and paid December 30. Confirm on the F/m Investments site, since schedules can change.

What is $TBIL's dividend yield?

$TBIL's 30-day SEC yield was 3.92% as of October 31, 2026, according to F/m Investments. Its trailing 12-month yield is about 3.66% based on the last 12 payments and the $49.93 price on November 13, 2026.

Is $TBIL or $SGOV better?

They pay about the same. $SGOV holds a basket of Treasury bills maturing in 0 to 3 months and charges 0.09%. $TBIL holds the newest 3-month Treasury bill and charges 0.15%. $SGOV is cheaper; $TBIL gives you a pure 3-month bill and pays at the end of the month instead of the start.
