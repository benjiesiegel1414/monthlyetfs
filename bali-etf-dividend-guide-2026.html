$BALI doesn't get talked about like $JEPI or $SPYI, and I think that's exactly why people search for it. It's a BlackRock fund, it pays monthly, it has around $1.4 billion in it, and it quietly does something most covered call funds can't: it has kept up with a big chunk of the S&P 500's gains while paying a real monthly check.

So let's walk through the actual numbers. Every 2026 payout, the pattern for ex-dates, why the yield you see depends on where you look, and how it compares with the funds you're probably already considering.

What Is $BALI?

$BALI is the iShares U.S. Large Cap Premium Income Active ETF. It launched on September 26, 2023, trades on Cboe BZX, charges 0.35% a year and had about $1.45 billion in net assets as of November 20, 2026, according to iShares.

It's run by the BlackRock Systematic team, and the strategy has two parts:

  • Stock selection. It holds a portfolio of large U.S. companies picked with BlackRock's quantitative models, benchmarked to the S&P 500. As of November 19, 2026 it held 223 positions.
  • Options for income. It sells call options to collect premium, and that premium is a big part of what gets paid out every month.

What's inside looks a lot like the S&P 500. The top holdings on iShares' November 19 holdings list were Apple (6.82%), Microsoft (5.49%), Nvidia (4.99%), Amazon (3.42%), Broadcom (2.33%), the two Alphabet share classes (about 4.2% combined), Berkshire Hathaway (2.04%) and Costco (1.82%). Tech was 32.6% of the fund.

Here's the part most people miss. $BALI isn't trying to squeeze out the biggest possible yield the way an old school buy-write fund like $XYLD does. iShares describes the goal as consistent income with lower volatility than the broader market, using both options and stock selection. Its 3 year equity beta was 0.82 and its 3 year standard deviation was 10.21% as of October 31, 2026. In plain English: it has moved less than the market, and as you'll see below, it has kept most of the market's upside.

Notice the January payment. Just like a lot of iShares funds, $BALI's "January" distribution goes ex-dividend at the end of December (December 30, 2025 last year). So if you count by ex-date, December has two and January has none. Count by the day the cash hits your account and you get one every month.

The range in 2026 so far: a low of $0.176748 in October and a high of $0.346138 in April. That April spike is not a typo, and I'll explain it in a second.

When Is the Next $BALI Dividend?

The pattern is very consistent. $BALI goes ex-dividend on the first business day of the month and pays about three to five business days later. Looking at the last 12 months, every regular ex-date landed on the 1st, or the next business day when the 1st fell on a weekend.

December 1, 2026 is a Tuesday, so the next ex-date should be around Tuesday, December 1, 2026, with cash a few days after. Then expect a second one near the end of December for the January check, like last year. iShares posts its official distribution schedule on the fund page, and the amount isn't known until it's declared. Anyone quoting you the December number today is guessing.

Why the Monthly Check Changes

$BALI is not a fixed payer. The distribution comes from stock dividends plus option premium, and option premium depends on how volatile the market is. When the market gets choppy, call options get more expensive, the fund collects more, and the payout tends to jump.

You can see it in the history. The two biggest payouts in the last two years were $0.38195 in May 2025 and $0.346138 in April 2026, both coming out of rough stretches for stocks. In calm months the check drifts back toward $0.18 to $0.22.

Year over year, the income has been fairly stable. The March through November 2026 payouts added up to $2.01 a share, versus $2.05 for the same nine months of 2025. Not growing, not shrinking much. That's what I'd expect from an options income fund: a steady-ish paycheck, not a dividend growth story like $DGRW.

How $BALI Dividends Are Taxed

iShares breaks each distribution into income, capital gains and return of capital once the year's numbers are final. For the 2025 payouts listed on the fund page (March through December 1, 2025), about 54% of each payment was classified as return of capital and the rest as income. The December 30, 2025 payment was all income.

Return of capital isn't automatically bad. For an options fund it often reflects how option gains and losses get accounted for, not the fund handing back your money. In a taxable account, ROC isn't taxed when you receive it. It lowers your cost basis, so you pay later when you sell. The 2026 breakdown won't be final until early 2027, so check your 1099.

Personally, I like option income funds like this in an IRA or Roth where none of it matters. In a taxable account, the ROC portion can actually work in your favor. This isn't tax advice, so talk to a tax pro about your own situation.

My Take

I've been a dividend investor for over 10 years, and I reached financial freedom at 31 by building dividend income. The thing I've learned the hard way is that the highest yield is rarely the best income fund. The best one is the fund whose share price holds up while it pays you.

That's what $BALI has done so far. A 6% to 7% forward payout, a 0.35% fee, no price decay and about 21% a year of total return since launch. You give up some yield versus $JEPI or $SPYI, and the check bounces around month to month, but you keep more of the market's growth.

Who it fits: someone who wants monthly income from big U.S. companies but doesn't want to fully cap their upside. Who it doesn't fit: someone who needs the exact same check every month, or who wants the highest yield possible. I'd rather own a fund like this than chase a 20% yielder that shrinks every year.

$BALI Dividend FAQ

Does $BALI pay a monthly dividend?

Yes. $BALI pays every month. It usually goes ex-dividend on the first business day of the month and pays a few business days later, with the January payment going ex-dividend at the end of December.

What is the $BALI dividend yield?

iShares lists a distribution rate of 6.89% as of November 19, 2026 and a 12 month trailing distribution rate of 7.84% as of October 31, 2026. The last 12 payouts add up to $2.66 a share, about 8.5% of the November 20 price, but that figure is boosted by one unusually large April payout.

What is the $BALI dividend history?

In 2026, $BALI's monthly payouts have ranged from $0.176748 to $0.346138 a share. The latest was $0.184278, ex-dividend November 2 and paid November 5, 2026. The March to November payouts totaled $2.01 a share in 2026 versus $2.05 in the same months of 2025.

When is the next $BALI ex-dividend date?

Based on its pattern, the next $BALI ex-dividend date should be around Tuesday, December 1, 2026, with a second December ex-date near month end for the January payment. iShares confirms dates and amounts on its distribution schedule.

Is $BALI a good investment for income?

It depends on your goals. $BALI pays a moderate 6% to 7% forward distribution, charges 0.35%, is not flagged for price decay on our table, and returned about 21% a year at NAV since its 2023 launch through September 2026. It pays less than $JEPI or $SPYI but has kept more of the S&P 500's growth. This is not financial advice.
