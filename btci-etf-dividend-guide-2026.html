People search "$BTCI dividend history" for one reason. They see a 30% yield on a bitcoin fund and they want to know if it's real, and if it lasts.

It's real. $BTCI has paid every single month since October 2024. But "does it last" is the part most people miss, and the dividend history answers it pretty clearly. So let's go through every payment, the dates, the taxes and the total return, and then I'll tell you how I actually think about a fund like this.

What Is $BTCI?

$BTCI is the NEOS Bitcoin High Income ETF. It launched on October 16, 2024, trades on Cboe, and as of October 30, 2026 had about $1.33 billion in net assets and 43.65 million shares outstanding. The total expense ratio is 0.99% (a 0.98% management fee plus 0.01% acquired fund fees).

NEOS is the same shop behind $QQQI and $SPYI, two of the most popular monthly payers out there. I broke those down in my $QQQI dividend guide and my $SPYI dividend guide. $BTCI is the same idea pointed at bitcoin: get exposure to the underlying, sell call options against it, and pay the premium out every month.

The difference is the underlying. The S&P 500 moves a few percent in a bad week. Bitcoin can move 20% in a bad week. That volatility is exactly why the yield is so big, and exactly why the payout swings so much.

How $BTCI Makes a 30% Yield

Here's the simple version. $BTCI doesn't buy bitcoin directly. It gets its bitcoin exposure through spot bitcoin ETFs and options, and it sells call options to generate income. Option buyers pay up for bitcoin calls because bitcoin is so volatile, and that premium is what funds your monthly check.

The holdings list on the NEOS site (as of November 2, 2026) shows how it's built:

  • U.S. Treasury bills, about 86% of the fund across two bills. This is the collateral that sits behind the option positions.
  • Spot bitcoin ETFs: $IBIT (iShares Bitcoin Trust) at about 17% and $HODL (VanEck Bitcoin ETF) at about 2%.
  • Index options on a bitcoin ETF index (listed as CBTX in the holdings). A long call paired with a short put at the same strike creates extra bitcoin exposure on top of the ETFs, and short calls are sold against it for income.

So the T-bills aren't the "safe part" of the fund in the way you'd think. They're there to back the options. The fund still moves a lot like bitcoin. Its beta was 1.46 on StockAnalysis, and that tells you this is not a bond fund in disguise.

NEOS also points out that index options are Section 1256 contracts, which get 60/40 long-term/short-term tax treatment. That's part of the tax story below.

Why the $BTCI Dividend Keeps Shrinking

Look at those numbers again. The biggest payment was $1.5718 in January 2025. The October 2026 payment was $0.6954. That's a drop of about 56%.

This is the part most people miss about high-yield option funds. The payout is not a fixed coupon. NEOS targets a distribution as a percentage of the share price, and that percentage depends on how much option premium bitcoin's volatility lets them collect. So when the share price falls, the dollar amount of the check falls with it, even if the yield percentage stays high.

And the share price did fall. $BTCI closed at $30.46 on October 30, 2026, versus a 52-week high of $56.76 and a 52-week low of $26.52. Bitcoin had a rough year, and $BTCI went along for the ride.

That's why I always tell people: don't just look at the yield, look at the dollar amount over time. A 30% yield on a shrinking share price can still mean a smaller check every year. That's what price decay looks like in practice, and it's exactly what I track for 130+ funds on TopDividendETFsPRO.

To be fair to $BTCI, the 2026 payments have been pretty stable since the spring, mostly between $0.63 and $0.80. If bitcoin recovers, the dollar payouts should rise with it. If bitcoin keeps sliding, they keep sliding.

$BTCI Taxes: Return of Capital

Here's a big one for taxable accounts. NEOS states that $BTCI's distributions have been classified as return of capital, and that they may be made up of option premiums, dividends, capital gains and interest.

Return of capital (ROC) generally isn't taxed when you receive it. Instead, it lowers your cost basis, so you pay tax later when you sell (or once your basis hits zero). Combined with the 60/40 treatment of Section 1256 index options, that's why NEOS funds tend to be popular in taxable accounts.

Two cautions. First, the monthly 19a-1 notices are only estimates, and the final breakdown comes at year end on your 1099 and the fund's Form 8937. Second, "return of capital" for tax purposes doesn't automatically mean the fund is handing your own money back to you. But when the share price is also falling a lot, like it did over the past year, it's worth being honest with yourself about where the income is really coming from. Talk to a tax pro about your own situation.

The Real Risks

  • Bitcoin risk, full stop. If bitcoin drops 40%, $BTCI is going down hard too. The premium softens it a little, that's it.
  • Shrinking dollar payouts. The monthly check fell from $1.57 to $0.70 in under two years. Plan your budget around that possibility.
  • Capped upside. In a big bitcoin rally, selling calls means you'll lag. Since inception it trailed the S&P Bitcoin Index by about 3 points a year.
  • Active, complex strategy. Synthetic exposure, index options, T-bill collateral. You're trusting NEOS to manage it well.
  • Short history and higher fee. Two years of data, and 0.99% is pricey next to a plain spot bitcoin ETF.

My Take

I've been a dividend investor for over 10 years, and I reached financial freedom at 31 by building income that actually lasts. So here's how I look at $BTCI.

It does what it says. It gives you bitcoin exposure with a big monthly check, and over its short life it has tracked bitcoin pretty closely after you count the dividends. If you already want bitcoin in your portfolio and you'd rather get paid monthly than hold $IBIT and wait, it's a reasonable way to do it.

What I wouldn't do is treat it as a core income holding. The income rides on bitcoin, and the history shows the check can get cut in half when bitcoin has a bad year. I'd rather build my base on steadier payers and keep something like $BTCI as a small satellite position, sized so that a 50% drop wouldn't change my life. And if you do own it, think hard about reinvesting part of the payout so you're not slowly spending your principal.

$BTCI Dividend FAQ

Does $BTCI pay dividends monthly?

Yes. $BTCI has paid a distribution every month since October 2024, 25 payments in total through October 2026. The ex-dividend date usually falls in the third or fourth week of the month and the payment arrives two days later.

When is $BTCI's next dividend date?

According to the NEOS distribution calendar, $BTCI's next ex-dividend date is November 18, 2026, payable November 20, 2026. The one after that is ex December 16, payable December 18, 2026. Amounts are announced around the declaration date.

How much is the $BTCI dividend?

The October 2026 $BTCI dividend was $0.6954 per share. Over the last 12 months $BTCI paid $9.53 per share, a trailing yield of about 31% on the October 30, 2026 price of $30.46. NEOS listed a 28.00% distribution rate as of September 30, 2026.

Why did the $BTCI dividend go down?

$BTCI's payout is tied to its share price and to how much option premium bitcoin's volatility allows. As bitcoin and the share price fell, the monthly payment dropped from $1.5718 in January 2025 to $0.6954 in October 2026.

Are $BTCI dividends taxed?

NEOS says $BTCI's distributions have been classified as return of capital, which generally lowers your cost basis instead of being taxed right away. The fund also uses Section 1256 index options. Final tax treatment comes on your 1099, so check with a tax professional.
